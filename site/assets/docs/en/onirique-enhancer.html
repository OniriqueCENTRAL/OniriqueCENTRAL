Overview

ONIRIQUE ENHANCER is a complete studio chain for vocals, wired in the order you’d patch it for real: a tube preamp, a console equalizer, a leveler, a flanger, a reverb plate and a limiter. A few knobs are enough: the vocal comes forward, warm and bright.

1. The tube preamp (TUBE PREAMP)

The first stage emulates a triode preamp. Here’s its electrical equivalent: the vocal arrives on the grid, the tube amplifies it towards the plate, and the bias point setting (cathode side) decides whether the tube saturates symmetrically or not.

What matters is the tube’s transfer curve : the relation between what goes in and what comes out. A straight line changes nothing in the sound; a curve that rounds off squashes the peaks and creates harmonics.

  • INPUT DRIVE pushes the vocal into the tube, up to +24 dB. The volume is made up : a vocal at −20 dBFS RMS keeps its level within 0.3 dB, from 0 to 100 %. The vocal gets denser, peaks are held and details come up, like in a real tube that compresses.
  • TUBE (TUBE DISTORTION) gives the character: more and more asymmetry, so more even harmonics.
  • BASS / AIR tilts the sound before the tube (two opposite shelves, below 250 Hz and above 3.5 kHz, up to 5 dB): it chooses what the tube colours, the body or the air.
  • No unwanted artifacts : saturation creates harmonics above 20 kHz, which fold back into the audible highs (aliasing). So the tube is computed four times faster than the sample rate, and through its antiderivative (antiderivative anti-aliasing): even pushed all the way, harmonics stay harmonics.
  • Behind the interface’s glass, the two tubes’ filaments light up when they’re working.

2. The console EQ (CONSOLE EQ)

Four fixed-frequency bands, chosen for vocals. As on an analog console, the more a band is pushed, the narrower it gets (its width follows its gain): a small setting stays broad and musical, a big setting stays precise.

  • BASS (shelf at 110 Hz, ±12 dB): the body of the vocal.
  • DE-MUD (dip at 320 Hz, down to −10 dB): removes what muddies a vocal, the “cardboard”.
  • TREBLE (bell at 3.2 kHz, ±12 dB): presence, intelligibility.
  • AIR (shelf at 12 kHz, −6 to +12 dB): breath, brightness.

3. The leveler

A very gentle compressor holds the vocal upfront, with no pumping: 3:1 ratio, 8 dB knee, threshold at −18 dBFS RMS, level measured over 5 ms, smoothed gain (3 ms attack, 100 ms release). INPUT LEVEL sets how hard it works: the louder the vocal comes in, the more it holds it.

4. Flanger and reverb

  • FLANGER : a slow stereo flanger (0.22 Hz), from a shimmer to a sweep. The vocal is blended with a copy of itself delayed by a few milliseconds, a delay that varies slowly: the comb filter’s notches move and open the vocal in stereo. The volume is held.
  • REVERB : ONIRIQUE VERB’s plate, tuned for vocals: low cut, sibilance removed before the reverb, and it ducks when you sing. It’s added to the vocal, which stays whole.

5. Limiter and MIX

  • OUTPUT LEVEL (−24 to +12 dB), then a look-ahead limiter : it sees each peak coming 1.5 ms ahead and lowers the gain just in time. The output never goes over −0.5 dBFS.
  • MIX blends the original vocal and the processed vocal. The original vocal is delayed by exactly the latency reported to the DAW (oversampling and limiter look-ahead, 2.75 ms). The filters are linear-phase and the delay lands exactly on the sample: at 50 %, no comb filtering, even in the highs. MIX 0 and BYPASS give back the exact original vocal.

Why it matters: two copies of a vocal offset by a fraction of a millisecond cancel each other at certain frequencies (comb effect, a “hollow”, metallic sound). ENHANCER aligns the two vocals to the sample so MIX never colours the sound.

Tips

Rap vocal upfront: INPUT DRIVE 40 %, TUBE 30 %, DE-MUD 40 %, TREBLE +3, AIR +4, REVERB 10 %.

Soft, warm vocal: BASS / AIR towards BASS 30, TUBE 60 %, AIR +2, FLANGER 15 % to open it up without doubling it.

The GR meter moves a lot: lower INPUT LEVEL; it’s made to remove a few dB, not to squash.
