Overview

ONIRIQUE DE-ESSER handles what hisses, pops or breathes in a vocal, in two ways. In AUTO, it’s a classic de-esser set with a THRESHOLD, working on its own during playback. In MANUAL (MANUEL on the plugin), it captures your take, cuts it into events (every S, every CH, every P, every breath) and you set each one by hand.

In AUTO, nothing is recorded. 33 ms of latency, compensated by your DAW, the same in both modes: switching never shifts anything. In AUTO, the LIVE box brings it down to 3 ms for recording through the plugin. Mono or stereo.

1. AUTO mode

One detector per sound, each on its own band: S (at the FREQ frequency), T (around 4.2 kHz), CH (around 3.2 kHz), F (the high hiss, around 9 kHz) and K (around 4 kHz). You switch on the ones you want to treat. When a band goes above the THRESHOLD, only that zone is turned down; the rest of the vocal doesn’t move.

Each detector only touches its own sound. The DE-ESSER listens to the first 30 ms of each sound before treating it: an attack that drops right away is a T or a K (the K drops above 9 kHz), a hiss that holds is an S, a CH or an F depending on its shape. Only that sound’s detector acts: with F alone switched on, an S stays untouched.

LIVE: for recording while singing through the plugin. Latency drops to 3 ms; without the 30 ms of listening, sorting is done on the first milliseconds of the sound, slightly less precise. Unticked, it’s the mix mode, the most accurate.

The detector also checks how much room the band takes in the sound: a bright voice has a lot of top end everywhere, and its vowels are not mistaken for S’s. A band has to weigh clearly more than usual in that voice to be turned down.

The view shows what is removed, sound by sound: the vocal level, the removed part in colour, and the letter of the sound turned down (S, T, CH, F, K) above each removal, with its reduction. The dotted line is the THRESHOLD: drag it right in the view; the thin line is the detected level.

  • RANGE: the strongest reduction allowed.
  • CLARITY: gives back brightness and air (a soft shelf from 3 kHz), but fades out while a sound is being turned down: the brightness comes back, not the S’s.
  • LISTEN: you only hear what is removed, to set FREQ and THRESHOLD by ear.
  • PAN S: moves only the sibilance (the S, CH and F you switched on) from left to right, as it goes by. The top end above 3 kHz goes to the side you pick, the rest of the voice stays in the centre, and detection doesn’t change. In the centre, the sound stays exactly the same; double-click to go back there. In MANUAL, the PAN DES SIBILANCES slider, under OPTIONS, does the same on every SIBILANCES event.

2. MANUAL mode

  1. Switch to MANUAL and play your take in the DAW: it records itself (CAPTURE AUTO), at its place in the project timeline, and is cut into events as it goes.
  2. Pick a family (SIBILANCES, CONSONNES for consonants, RESPIRATIONS for breaths): its events show up.
  3. Hover an event to hear it, drag it down to lower it.

What is already recorded is not recorded again at every playback. As soon as it hears something new (a track added further on, another take at the same spot), it adds it and cuts it into events; the events you already set keep their settings. The CAPTURE AUTO button tells you what it’s doing (ENREGISTRE… recording, À JOUR up to date); one click turns it off or back on.

During playback, every event you set is turned down as a whole for its duration, like clip gain drawn by hand, with a 5 ms fade (20 ms for a breath): no click, no filter, the sound stays your voice. Outside the events, nothing changes.

Two views: COLONNES (columns), every event of the family side by side (their sizes compare at a glance, the former size stays as an outline when you lower one), and TIMELINE, the whole take with every sound in place and the playhead.

TOUT RÉDUIRE (reduce all) lowers a whole family at once: the most salient ones (those that stick out most from their phrase) more. An event set by hand no longer moves when you change TOUT RÉDUIRE; a double-click hands it back.

Event settings are kept in your project. The captured take is saved separately on your computer (ONIRIQUE DE-ESSER/Captures folder): on another computer, the settings still apply, only the waveforms are missing.

Tips

Find your S’s: in AUTO, switch LISTEN on and turn FREQ until you only hear the hiss, then lower the THRESHOLD until S letters show up on the S’s, and nowhere else.

A lead take: in MANUAL, TOUT RÉDUIRE at 40 % on sibilance, then go through the columns one by one with the arrows: fix by hand the two or three S’s that still sting.

Breaths: TOUT RÉDUIRE at 50–70 % lowers them without cutting them; a vocal with no breath at all sounds fake.

If you change the take in your DAW, just replay the passage: its events are redone, the others keep their settings.
