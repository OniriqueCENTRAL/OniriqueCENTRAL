Overview

ONIRIQUE HALO is a high-frequency limiter. It only processes one frequency band, the one you choose with two flags on the analyzer, and leaves the rest of the sound exactly as it came in. Sibilance, hi-hats, cymbals or the harsh top of a mix are held in check without dulling the brightness.

The sound is split in two: the band, which goes through the limiter, and the rest, which passes unprocessed. The two are recombined exactly: at rest, HALO gives back the sound unchanged, down to the sample in LINEAR.

1. The band and its flags

Two flags, low and high, are dragged on the analyzer between 50 Hz and 20 kHz. They always stay at least half an octave apart. The processed zone lights up in red on the analyzer, exactly between them.

The band comes in three shapes:

  • SPLIT : everything above the low flag (high flag all the way up). A 24 dB per octave cut.
  • DEFINED : only what lies between the two flags, 24 dB per octave on each side.
  • FULL HF : a gentle 12 dB per octave slope above the low flag. It holds the whole top of the spectrum more naturally, with no hard boundary.

Dragging the high flag below 19.5 kHz switches to DEFINED; taking it back to the top returns to SPLIT.

2. The limiter

The band is limited sample by sample : when it goes over the threshold, it’s brought back under. The threshold is set on the large fader, from 0 to −40 dB, with two separate thresholds in stereo (see below).

No millisecond times to set. Timing is program-dependent: the more gain reduction, the faster the limiter reacts. A slight overshoot is handled gently, a violent sibilant is caught at once. Two independent switches set the character:

  • ATTACK SLOW or FAST: how fast the reduction comes in.
  • RELEASE SLOW or FAST: how fast it lets go.

Detection looks a short moment ahead (in LINEAR and DYNAMIC): the gain is already down when the peak arrives.

TRIM (±6 dB) raises or lowers the band without changing the amount of limiting : the threshold sets the dynamics, TRIM the tonal balance.

3. Stereo and sidechain

  • L/R or M/S : in L/R, one threshold for left, one for right; in M/S, one for the centre (MID), one for the sides (SIDE). The two thresholds can be linked. In M/S, you can tame sibilance in the centre without dulling the air on the sides.
  • LINK (0 – 100 %): at 100 %, both channels get the same reduction, the image doesn’t move; at 0 %, each is limited on its own.
  • EXT (sidechain): another track routed to the external input drives the detection of MAIN, PARALIMIT, or both. A vocal can then carve the highs of a beat only while it sings.

4. PARALIMIT

PARALIMIT is a second band, in parallel, with its own flags. It’s heavily limited, then its level is made up automatically (up to +24 dB) and it’s added to the sound. The result: presence and air, dense and even, without harshness.

  • AMOUNT (−∞ to +12 dB): the amount added.
  • Threshold (0 to −60 dB) and TRIM (±6 dB), as for MAIN.
  • Channel : M (the centre), S (the sides) or L/R. Limiting always happens on the true stereo, before the centre or sides are extracted: the image doesn’t move.
  • A click in the PARALIMIT zone of the analyzer switches it on.

5. Processing modes and latency

ModeLatency at 48 kHzFor
LINEAR≈ 89 msLinear-phase split: no phase colouring, exact null at rest. Mastering.
DYNAMIC1.5 to 2.9 msIIR split and slight look-ahead: transients are caught. Mixing.
LIVE0 to 9 samplesNo look-ahead, near-zero latency: recording or playing through.

The latency isn’t a guess: HALO measures it itself on the active chain, and reports it to your DAW, which keeps everything aligned. The ⚙ menu shows the table of measured latencies.

OS (1x to 8x) oversamples the limiter during playback; RENDER picks another factor for export.

6. Listening

  • S under the flags: solos the whole selected band. Right-click: solo only while you hold.
  • DELTA : hear only what HALO removes.
  • MATCH : compensates the volume, to compare at equal level.
  • Reference (⚙ menu): a tonal balance curve behind the analyzer (pink noise, modern mix, vocal, or a curve you capture).

Tips

Vocal sibilance : DEFINED between 5 and 10 kHz, ATTACK FAST, RELEASE SLOW, then lower the threshold while listening in solo (S) until the S’s settle. Go back to normal listening to judge.

A mix top end that’s too harsh : FULL HF from 3 or 4 kHz, in LINEAR, 1 to 2 dB of reduction is enough. PARALIMIT on S, with a little AMOUNT, brings back the air on the sides without hardening it.

To compare fairly, turn on MATCH: a louder sound always seems better.
