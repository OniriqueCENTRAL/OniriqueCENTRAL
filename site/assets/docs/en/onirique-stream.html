What it’s for

When your DAW runs on your sound card’sASIO driver, Windows no longer hears anything it plays: so Discord can’t share your session’s sound. ONIRIQUE STREAM makes a copy of your master’s sound and sends it to a Windows device (the virtual cable VB-CABLE), while your DAW stays on ASIO, with no change to what you hear.

Setup and use

  1. Put STREAM last on the master.
  2. OUTPUT DEVICE : “CABLE Input (VB-Audio Virtual Cable)”, picked automatically if it’s installed. No cable? INSTALL VB-CABLE downloads the official pack, checks VB-Audio’s signature, installs it with Windows’ permission and selects it.
  3. In Discord, share the DAW window with sound on. Discord takes the sound of the DAW process, so the one STREAM sends.
  • Another device (speakers…) works too, but you’ll hear it on top of ASIO: the status shows it.
  • Your settings are saved in the project and on this PC: a new STREAM picks up the last device, the buffer, START / STOP and ECHO GUARD. An unplugged device is reopened automatically when it comes back.
  • Only one instance sends at a time (no doubled sound if STREAM is on two tracks), and nothing is sent during an export.

The clock bridge

Your sound card and the virtual cable each have their own clock. Even set to the same rate, they never tick at exactly the same pace: a few samples of difference per second are enough for a buffer to end up overflowing (click) or running dry (dropout). The bridge handles this like a tank whose level is watched.

  • The regulator starts fast (locked in a few seconds) then gradually slows down: the correction becomes almost still, with no audible modulation.
  • Different rates (DAW at 44.1 kHz, cable at 48 kHz): the base ratio handles it, and the filter cuts below the new limit to avoid any aliasing.
  • Empty tank (transport stopped, output started before the DAW): fade to silence, then restarts on its own.
  • The margin adapts: if the reserve stays large for 10 s, latency drops a little; each dropout raises it back.

ECHO GUARD

If the stream’s sound comes back into your DAW (an input recording the cable, “Stereo Mix”…), it goes through STREAM again, back into the cable, back again… and the echo spirals into feedback. ECHO GUARD cuts the stream before that happens. Your DAW’s sound is never touched.

  • Each millisecond of the input is brought to the same power before the comparison: a loud cymbal doesn’t drown the signature, and quiet moments count fully.
  • A real loop keeps the same delay from one measurement to the next; chance doesn’t. A loop confirmed three times at the same delay cuts the stream.
  • Repeating music (loops, choruses) can’t trigger the guard: only the signature counts.
  • RE-ARM restarts the stream once the loop is removed.

FAQ

My friends hear nothing: check that you’re sharing the DAW window (not the whole screen) with sound on, and that STREAM is on START with CABLE Input selected.

The stream cut out on its own: that’s ECHO GUARD. Look for a track or an input recording the cable or “Stereo Mix”, remove it, then RE-ARM.

I hear myself twice: the selected output isn’t the cable but a device you’re listening to. Switch back to CABLE Input.
