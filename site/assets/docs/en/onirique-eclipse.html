Overview

ONIRIQUE ECLIPSE is a parallel Mid / Side mastering equalizer. Put it on the mix bus, a vocal bus or a beat, to make everything sound bigger: warmth in the lows, air up top, density in the middle.

The sound is first split into centre (Mid) and sides (Side). Each goes through the same band network, then everything is recombined into left / right. Processing runs at twice the sample rate, for faithful highs right up to the top of the spectrum.

1. Parallel equalization

On a classic equalizer, the bands are chained: each filter processes the previous one’s output. In ECLIPSE, every band receives the same signal and only returns a difference, which is added to the original sound. It’s the principle of passive mastering equalizers.

  • When you push several bands, they blend instead of stacking up : the mix stays open even with bold settings.
  • A band at neutral returns nothing at all. All at neutral, ECLIPSE returns exactly the original sound, with no phase rotation.
  • The displayed curve is computed by the same function as the processing: what you see is what you hear.

2. The bands

LOW

A low shelf from 20 to 400 Hz, with separate BOOST and ATTEN controls (0 to 12 dB each) on the same frequency. Their two curves don’t have the same shape: pushed together, the lows rise and the low-mids just above dip, the famous trick of passive equalizers. BANDWIDTH sets the width.

MID

A bell from 150 Hz to 8 kHz, ±12 dB, with its BANDWIDTH (Q). Two small bells of opposite sign, on either side, follow it proportionally: the band sounds like an analog circuit, not a mathematical bell.

CONTOUR

It reshapes the mids in one move, from 200 Hz to 6 kHz, ±10 dB. Positive, it dips the centre and lifts both flanks (at a third and at three times the frequency); negative, the opposite. BANDWIDTH spreads the flanks, from 1 to 2.3 octaves. Perfect for removing the “cardboard” side of a mix without hollowing it out.

HIGH

A high shelf from 1.5 to 24 kHz, mirroring LOW: separate BOOST and ATTEN controls, BANDWIDTH.

HPF and LPF

These aren’t phase-rotating low cuts, but −24 dB shelves at 12 dB per octave, with no resonance: useless lows or harsh highs are lowered gently. HPF from 10 to 400 Hz, LPF from 3 to 30 kHz.

3. Mid / Side and OFFSET

A single set of controls drives the centre and the sides. With M/S LINK, the two are identical. Without the link, each band’sOFFSET (±12 dB, ±10 for CONTOUR) shifts the setting:

  • SIDE : the offset applies to the sides. Add air on the sides, or keep your 808 in the centre.
  • MID : the offset applies to the centre.
  • SPLIT : half the offset on the centre, the other half reversed on the sides.

4. Analog colour

  • ANALOG and DRIVE (character): the more a band pushes, the more it colours, each in its own way. With ANALOG off, the network stays perfectly clean.
  • XFORMER (0 – 100 %): the input and output transformers. They thicken and saturate mostly the lows, when pushed.

5. Listening and image

  • SOLO M / SOLO S : listen to the centre or the sides alone.
  • DELTA : hear only what ECLIPSE adds or removes.
  • MONO : checks mono compatibility.
  • WIDTH (0 – 200 %): the image width, on the sides.
  • AUTO GAIN : compensates the volume (K-weighting) to judge the equalization at equal level.

Tips

The low-end trick : LOW at 60 Hz, BOOST at 4 and ATTEN at 2. The sub gains weight, the muddy low-mids dip just above.

A bigger mix : HIGH BOOST at 12 kHz, then positive SIDE OFFSET on HIGH and negative on LOW. The air opens up on the sides, the lows stay in the centre.

Judge at equal level with AUTO GAIN, and check in MONO from time to time.
