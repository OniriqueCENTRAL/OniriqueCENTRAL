Overview

ONIRIQUE F76 is a FET compressor: very fast, punchy, full of character. It keeps a vocal up front, gives a snare its snap, tightens a bass. The front follows studio racks: big INPUT and OUTPUT, ATTACK and RELEASE stacked, the RATIO column, the VU and the METER column.

Mono or stereo. The latency is the one of the chosen oversampling, reported to the DAW.

1. Driving the compressor

As on the hardware, the threshold is fixed: INPUT sets how much it compresses. The harder you push, the more it compresses; OUTPUT makes up the level.

ATTACK goes from 800 µs (fully left) to 20 µs (fully right), RELEASE from 1.1 s to 50 ms: to the right is faster. The release depends on the program, like the machine: it comes back slower after long compression.

2. RATIO and ALL

4, 8, 12 or 20: higher holds harder. ALL is every button pushed in at once: the ratio climbs, the knee bends and the grit arrives. That’s the crush of drums and aggressive vocals.

3. BLACK and BLUE

Just above the VU. BLACK: the clean, tight version. BLUE: the first generation, more coloured, with more grit and forward mids. The version only changes the sound, not the settings.

4. MIX and OVERSAMPLING

  • MIX: the dry sound blended with the compressed one, for parallel compression. The dry sound is delayed exactly like the compressed one: no phase dip, at any setting.
  • OVERSAMPLING: OFF, 2x, 4x or 8x. The compressor runs faster and the top end stays clean even under heavy compression. The new latency is reported to the DAW.
  • METER: the VU shows gain reduction (GR) or the output level (+8, +4), or turns off (OFF).

Tips

A rap vocal up front: RATIO 8, ATTACK around 5, RELEASE around 6, INPUT up to 6 to 8 dB of reduction on the peaks.

Crushed drums: ALL, ATTACK and RELEASE at their fastest, INPUT high, then MIX around 40 % to keep the transients.

Before L2A: F76 catches the peaks fast, ONIRIQUE L2A then levels gently. The classic studio vocal chain.
