Overview

ONIRIQUE TUNER does three things, in this order: it listens to the vocal to find out which note it’s singing, it decides which note to play instead, then it moves the vocal to that note without changing its timbre. All of it happens sample by sample, on one voice at a time (monophonic).

LIVE or HQ

Both modes use the same engine, but don’t place the grains the same way:

  • LIVE predicts where the next period will fall from the ones already heard. It doesn’t need a single sample ahead: zero latency, ideal for recording with the corrected vocal in your headphones.
  • HQ delays the vocal to measure each period on both sides of each grain, and smooths the pitch curve by seeing what’s coming. More precise and cleaner on fast notes; the latency is reported to the DAW, which compensates it in the mix.

Changing mode or range (INPUT TYPE) reconfigures the whole engine: TUNER does it inside a very short output fade, never abruptly.

1. Pitch detection

Before measuring, the vocal goes through an analysis path that you never hear: a high-pass below the lowest possible note (it removes rumble), a 4th-order Butterworth low-pass, then decimation (fewer samples are kept, which makes the measurement faster without losing anything useful).

The detector uses the YIN method: it compares the signal with itself shifted by every possible duration, and looks for the shift where the vocal resembles itself most. That shift is the period (the inverse of the frequency). The computation goes through an FFT for speed, and returns a fractional period, so finer than one sample.

  • INPUT TYPE (Soprano, Alto / Tenor, Baritone / Bass, Instrument, Bass Inst) sets the search range: the closer it fits the vocal, the less the detector can get the octave wrong.
  • Once a note is locked, the detector follows it: it doesn’t let go on a doubtful measurement, and only changes octave on evidence that lasts several measurements.
  • TRACKING sets how strict it is: low, it accepts a breathy or noisy vocal; high, it only corrects what is clearly a note.
  • Anything far below the vocal’s recent level (headphone bleed, reverb tail) is left as it is: these are in-tune notes, but too quiet to be corrected.

2. Correction: which note to play

The corrector continuously picks the note of the scale (KEY + SCALE) closest to the vocal, then brings the vocal onto it. The speed of that move is RETUNE SPEED.

Electrically, the correction behaves like a one-pole RC filter : the target note is the input voltage, the produced pitch is the capacitor voltage. RETUNE SPEED is the R×C time constant. A one-pole filter never overshoots its target: the correction never exaggerates the vocal’s vibrato.

The other settings act on that move:

  • AMOUNT : only makes up part of the gap to the note (100 % = the exact note, 50 % = halfway).
  • FLEX-TUNE : only corrects when the vocal is already approaching the note; slides and notes deliberately “in between” get through.
  • HUMANIZE : lengthens the correction time on the held part of long notes, so they don’t sound frozen.
  • SUSTAIN : acts on the held part only. To the left, looser; to the right, straighter, without changing the arrival on the note.
  • NATURAL VIBRATO : enlarges or reduces the vocal’s vibrato before correction (from −12 to +12).
  • Keyboard : each note can be removed from or added to the scale, or bypassed (the vocal closest to it passes uncorrected).
  • Vibrato threshold (Wide / Narrow): decides what counts as vibrato rather than a real movement of the voice.
  • Transitions : when the vocal leaves its note for another, the correction freezes for the slide, then resumes on arrival. The move from one note to the next keeps the singer’s gesture.

Created vibrato

The VIBRATO button adds a vibrato independent of the singer’s, after correction: shape (sine, triangle, square, sawtooth), speed (RATE), depth in cents (DEPTH), delay before it starts (DELAY), rise time (ONSET) and VARIATION from one cycle to the next. AMPLITUDE makes the volume follow, and TIMBRE the brightness (a bell around 1.5 kHz), like a real voice whose vibrato also moves the sound.

3. Grain-based pitch shifting (PSOLA)

To change the pitch without speeding the vocal up or turning it into a cartoon voice, TUNER cuts the vocal into grains : small pieces two periods long, centred on each cycle of the voice and softened by a bell-shaped window. To raise the note, it moves the grains closer together ; to lower it, it moves them further apart. Each grain keeps its shape: the timbre stays the singer’s.

  • The windows complement each other exactly: the sum of two neighbouring grains stays at the same level, with no “pumping”.
  • More grains per second means more energy: TUNER compensates so the perceived volume doesn’t follow the pitch shift.
  • FORMANT on: each grain keeps the size of two periods of the original vocal, whatever the spacing between grains. The mouth’s resonances (the formants) don’t move: a vocal raised by a fifth doesn’t turn into a child’s voice.
  • THROAT (60 to 160 %) lengthens or shortens the “throat”: it deliberately moves the formants, for a darker or thinner voice.
  • TRANSPOSE shifts everything from −12 to +12 semitones; DETUNE changes the A reference (shown in Hz around 440).

4. The voice-driven vocoder

A classic vocoder needs a keyboard. TUNER’s doesn’t: the notes come from the vocal itself. The corrector turns the vocal into notes (a new note has to be confirmed over two measurements, so notes you only pass through aren’t played), and those notes play an internal synthesizer.

  • VOICING : what the synth plays around the sung note. Mono (the note alone), Octave, Fifth, Chord (a chord built in the key).
  • BLEND : from the tuned vocal alone (0 %) to the vocoder alone (100 %).
  • On a consonant or a breath, the carrier becomes noise: the words stay understandable.
  • Level safeguard: the vocoder never comes out louder than the vocal.
  • MIDI output : the derived notes also come out as MIDI. Route them to a synth in your DAW to double the vocal with any instrument.

Tips

Strong “autotune” effect: HARD TUNE, or RETUNE SPEED at 0–5 ms, HUMANIZE at 0. Pick the scale carefully: an out-of-scale note jumps to its neighbour.

Invisible correction: HQ mode, RETUNE SPEED between 40 and 80 ms, FLEX-TUNE around 30 %, HUMANIZE 30 to 50 %, FORMANT on.

Recording with the correction in your headphones: LIVE mode (zero latency), then switch to HQ for the mix.

The correction gets the octave wrong or hesitates: set INPUT TYPE to your range, raise TRACKING, and put TUNER before the reverb and delay.
