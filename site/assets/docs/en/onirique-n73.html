Overview

ONIRIQUE N73 is a preamp-equalizer in the spirit of the British console module from the 70s (the 1073): mic or line input, stepped gain, three-band equalizer and high-pass filter. Its console grain comes from the circuit itself: the transformers, the amplifier stages and the EQ networks are simulated from the original schematics.

The pots act on the real resistors of the networks: that’s why the shape of the curves changes with the setting, just like on the unit.

1. Input and gain

  • MIC and LINE are two ranges of the same knob. Clicking MIC or LINE switches input and recalls that input’s last position.
  • MIC: from −20 to −80, through the input transformer. The higher the gain, the thicker the lows and harmonics get.
  • MIC Z: the impedance the mic sees, LO (300 Ω) or HI (1.2 kΩ). It slightly changes the lows and the presence.
  • PAD: −20 dB before the mic input.
  • OFF: processing stops and the sound passes as is.

EQL puts the equalizer in or out. Even flat, the network keeps its slight signature (±0.4 dB), like the module.

3. Fader and output

  • FADER: the console fader, before the output amplifier, from −∞ to +10 dB (unity at 0). Above 0 dB it pushes the output amp and its transformer: that’s where the grain rises.
  • OUTPUT: the final volume, −24 to +12 dB, clean, no effect on saturation.
  • PHASE: flips the polarity.
  • POWER: off, the original sound.

4. Modern settings

  • CAL: the analogue level matching 0 dBFS, from +18 to +26 dBu (+26 by default). The lower it is, the harder your signal hits the module.
  • LEVEL MATCH: compensates the volume, to judge grain and EQ at equal loudness.
  • QUALITY: Normal or High (higher internal rate, more faithful in the extreme highs).

Tips

Console grain on a vocal: MIC input, raise the GAIN until you hear the lows thicken, then bring the level back with OUTPUT. Turn on LEVEL MATCH to compare honestly.

Classic presence: MID at 3.2 or 4.8 kHz, +3 to +5 dB, HIGH +2 dB, HIGH PASS at 80 Hz.

More saturation: lower CAL towards +18 dBu, or push the FADER above 0.
